# Chess: 800 → 1500 Lichess Rapid by end of year

You've given me enough that I'm not going to pepper you with questions — I'll commit to a plan and flag the 2–3 places where your honest answer changes it. Read those and self-correct if they apply.

## The short version (read this twice)

At 800, you will not gain rating by learning openings, watching streamers, or reading books. You will gain rating by **doing tactics puzzles daily and playing longer time-control games with post-game analysis**. Everything else is garnish. I'll tell you what garnish to add and when — but if you only do two things from this document, do those two.

The realistic path from 800 → 1500 in 8–9 months on 30–45 min/day exists, but it's on the aggressive end. It happens if you execute a focused daily session, not if you dabble. I'll tell you what to cut.

## What the goal actually requires

**Goal:** Hit 1500 Lichess Rapid (15+10 or 10+5) by end of December. That translates roughly to:

- You stop hanging pieces in one-move tactics. (This alone gets you to ~1200.)
- You reliably spot 2–3 move tactics when they appear. (This gets you to ~1400.)
- You have a rough idea what you're doing in the opening, a basic endgame toolkit (K+P vs K, basic checkmates, the opposition), and you don't fall apart when the position gets quiet. (This gets you to ~1500.)

Notice the ordering. The non-negotiable foundation is **not losing material**. If you skip to "learning openings" or "studying grandmaster games" before that foundation is solid, you will plateau around 1100 and blame the wrong thing.

## Where you are now (the frontier)

From what you told me:
- **Solid:** rules, basic piece movement, basic tactical patterns (pin, fork) as concepts.
- **Shaky:** recognizing those patterns fast enough to use them in a real game with a clock running. Spotting them when you're the one who could *be forked*. Recognizing the other 8–10 common tactical motifs (skewer, discovered attack, removal of the defender, deflection, back-rank, overloaded piece, zwischenzug, double attack).
- **Probably missing entirely:** endgame technique (even king+queen vs king is slow for most 800s), an actual decision procedure for picking a move, any sense of what makes a good/bad move in quiet positions.

That diagnosis drives the whole plan.

## The phased roadmap (8 months, April → December)

Phases are 3–5 weeks each with a visible milestone. Each phase has a target rating *band*, not a single number — rating is noisy and a bad week doesn't mean the plan is broken.

### Phase 1 — "Stop hanging stuff" (weeks 1–4, target: 800 → 1000)

The single highest-value skill at your level: before every move, check what your opponent is attacking and what would be loose if you move the piece you're about to move. This is called "board vision" / "blunder check" and it's ~80% of the gap between 800 and 1100.

- Drill: one-move tactics puzzles (Lichess puzzle rush / puzzle storm / puzzle training filtered to rating ~800–1200). **Hundreds of them.** Not for theory. For pattern recognition in under 5 seconds.
- Force a 2-step routine on every move in real games: (1) "what is my opponent threatening?" (2) "if I make this move, what's suddenly undefended?" This feels insultingly slow at first. Keep doing it.
- Endgame basics this phase: K+Q vs K mate, K+R vs K mate, the opposition with K+P vs K.

**Milestone (end of week 4):** Beat a Lichess bot at level 3 without losing material. Lichess rating stable above 950 across at least 20 rapid games.

### Phase 2 — "See the tactics" (weeks 5–10, target: 1000 → 1200)

Broaden tactical vocabulary. Puzzles at your actual puzzle rating — Lichess puzzles are rated separately from games, and your puzzle rating is probably already 1200–1400 because puzzles are easier than games. Use that.

- Start Lichess's "Practice" section for tactical motifs one at a time: pin → skewer → fork → discovered attack → removal of the defender → double attack → back-rank → overloaded piece. One motif per 2–3 days. Then blocks of mixed (interleaved) puzzles at your puzzle rating.
- Pick a simple opening repertoire and stop changing it. As White: 1. e4 and play the Italian (Giuoco Piano) against everything symmetrical and a basic scheme against the Sicilian / Caro / French / Scandi. As Black: play the Caro-Kann against e4 and the London-mirror or King's Indian setup against d4. This is not "studying openings" — this is **memorizing the first 4–6 moves so you stop burning clock and getting bad positions in the first 3 minutes**. Twenty minutes total on each side, once.
- Endgame: rook endgames basics (Lucena, Philidor — not deep, just recognize the positions).

**Milestone (end of week 10):** Lichess rapid rating stable above 1150 across 30+ games. Puzzle rating ~1700+.

### Phase 3 — "Don't fall apart in quiet positions" (weeks 11–18, target: 1200 → 1350)

Around 1200–1300 the other person also stops hanging pieces and you now have to actually make decisions in positions without tactics. This is where most people plateau. The fix is **studying your own games with an engine** and **learning a small set of positional ideas** (outposts, open files, weak squares, good vs. bad bishop, pawn structure basics).

- Post-game analysis becomes mandatory: after every rapid game you lose (not just blunders — every loss), run it through the Lichess engine and find the *first* move that changed your win probability by 20+ points. Write it in a notebook: "move X, position Y, I didn't see Z." One line. This list is gold.
- Start the weekly ritual of replaying an annotated master game with a book or YouTube series (*John Bartholomew's Climbing the Rating Ladder* or Daniel Naroditsky's *Speedrun* series at the 1200–1400 level is the standard recommendation; pick one and stay).
- Keep tactics puzzles daily, but at a slightly higher difficulty. 10–15 min/day is enough once you're past the motif-learning phase.

**Milestone (end of week 18):** Rating stable above 1300. Your "games I lost" notebook has 40+ entries, grouped by pattern.

### Phase 4 — "Consolidate to 1500" (weeks 19–30, target: 1350 → 1500)

- Deeper endgame study (pawn endgames properly, basic opposite-color and same-color bishop endings, the lucena and philidor at real depth).
- Re-study the notebook from phase 3 monthly — look for repeating weaknesses (I always lose on the kingside when I castle short and push h3 too early; I keep missing back-rank threats with 90 seconds on the clock). Every repeated pattern becomes a targeted puzzle theme for 2 weeks.
- Slight opening widening: learn the *ideas* behind your openings (not more lines) — what you're trying to do with the bishops, where the break comes from, what the middlegame plan is.
- One "slow game" per week (30+0 or 30+20), fully focused, phone away. This is the one where you actually practice the decision procedure at full depth.

**Milestone (end of December):** 1500 Lichess Rapid, stable across ~30 games.

## The daily session (30–45 min)

This is the template. The minute allocations assume 40 min; scale to your day.

**(a) Warm-up / spaced review — 5 min.**
Five puzzles at your current puzzle rating, from memory, untimed. Don't worry about score. This wakes up pattern recognition before you play.

**(b) Focused tactical training — 15 min.**
- In phase 1: one-move hang-spotting puzzles (Lichess puzzle "themes" filtered to "mate in 1" and "hanging piece"). Aim for accuracy, not speed.
- In phases 2+: a block of puzzles on the current phase's motif, then a mixed block. Always check the ones you get wrong — replay the position, try to find the move again 10 minutes later.
- Keep a running list of "puzzles I failed" — if the same motif keeps showing up, that's the next week's focus.

**(c) Play a real game — 15–20 min.**
**One** Lichess Rapid game at 10+5 or 15+10. One. Not ten blitz games, not five 3+0 games. Blitz and bullet at your level will actively make you worse — they reward the pattern-matching you already have and punish the careful blunder-check you're trying to build.

**(d) Analyze the game — 5–10 min.**
Run the engine. Find the first big mistake. One line in your notebook: "move X, I played Y, engine says Z, reason: I didn't see [pattern]." Done. Do not spend 40 minutes on analysis; 5–10 is enough and repeatable.

**Total: 30–45 minutes.**

**If you're really short on time (bad day):** do 10 min of puzzles and one game you don't analyze. Keep the streak alive. It's the sustainability that compounds, not any single session.

**If you have a longer weekend block (90 min):** do the weekly ritual — one slow rapid game (30+0 or 30+20) with phone away, analyzed in depth (20–30 min of analysis, not 5). This is where the biggest jumps come from.

## Ratio check

Your session is roughly:
- ~15% passive (today that's basically zero — maybe a little opening prep or studying a master game on weekends)
- ~60–70% active production (puzzles + playing)
- ~15–25% review + analysis (warm-up puzzles + engine review)

That is the ratio that works. **The temptation at your level is to increase the passive percentage — watch streamers, read a book, watch GothamChess.** Resist. Watching other people play chess feels like learning and is mostly not. If you want one passive thing, swap it in on the weekend slow-game day and keep weekdays active.

## Resources (one primary per category, please)

- **Puzzles:** Lichess puzzle training. Free, adaptive, you already have an account. Don't also use Chesstempo and ChessTactics.pro and Chess.com — pick one. Lichess is fine.
- **Games:** Lichess Rapid at 10+5 or 15+10. That's it.
- **Opening scheme:** Lichess opening explorer + Chessly's / ChessMood's / Hanging Pawns' free YouTube series on a specific repertoire. Pick one set and stop shopping. **Twenty total minutes of opening study per phase is enough** through 1500. Seriously.
- **Endgame:** Lichess endgame practice module (it's built in) covers K+Q, K+R, pawn endings, the opposition. Free. Sufficient through 1500.
- **Instructional:** *one* YouTube series for weekend viewing. Options that are at the right level: Daniel Naroditsky's *Speedrun* (start at the 1000 level and go up with him), John Bartholomew's *Chess Fundamentals* and *Climbing the Rating Ladder*. Pick one. Stick with it.
- **Book (optional, only if you like reading):** *Logical Chess: Move by Move* by Irving Chernev. One annotated game = one week's weekend material. Do NOT buy five books.

Notice what's missing: streamers (Hikaru, GothamChess, Levy), openings-specific courses, puzzle books. All fine in small doses, none of them are the plan.

## Feedback and assessment

- **Per-puzzle feedback:** Lichess shows you the answer immediately. When you get one wrong, don't just click through — spend 30 seconds replaying the position and finding the idea.
- **Per-game feedback:** Post-game engine review after every rapid game. One-line journal entry for every loss.
- **Weekly check:** Friday or Sunday — look at the week's journal. Any pattern that shows up 3+ times is next week's puzzle theme.
- **Phase milestones:** the per-phase rating targets above. These are soft — aim to be stable (not peak) at that rating across ~30 games before declaring the phase done. Rating is noisy; stability matters.

## Accountability (do not skip this — it's the biggest predictor of whether you're at 1500 in December)

You're an adult doing this for fun, which is *exactly* the profile where plans fizzle out in month 2. Pick one of these, this week:

1. **(Best) A cheap coach, once every 2 weeks, for 30 min.** Chess coaches at your level are cheap ($20–40/session on iTalki-equivalents like Chessable/ChessCoachBox, or sometimes cheaper from titled players in lower cost-of-living countries on Fiverr). One session every 2 weeks is ~$40/month and replaces about 10 hours of self-directed puzzle-floundering. If budget allows, this is the single highest-leverage addition to the plan.
2. **(Very good) A chess-improvement partner at similar rating.** A friend or someone from the /r/chessbeginners Discord or Lichess team. You both commit to a weekly 30-min voice-chat game + review. The anticipatory effect (you study more because someone's watching on Sunday) is real.
3. **(Decent) Public streak + milestone dates.** Tell one specific person your milestones (end of week 4: 1000; end of June: 1150; etc.). Lichess streaks visible to them. Weaker than (1) or (2) but better than nothing.

Don't leave this as "I'll figure it out." Pick one by Friday.

## Pivot signals — read this, bookmark it

A plan is a hypothesis. Check it against reality. If any of these fire, adjust; don't grind on and don't bail.

- **Missed the week-4 milestone (not stable at 950+).** The issue is almost certainly that you're still hanging material — the "blunder check" routine isn't ingrained. Cut new things for 2 weeks; do *only* one-move tactics and the blunder-check drill. Come back to the main plan.
- **Stuck at 1200 for 3+ weeks in phase 3.** This is the quiet-position plateau and it's normal for ~3 weeks. If it's 4+ weeks, the fix is more post-game analysis (not more puzzles) — puzzles are solving a problem you've already mostly solved at that rating.
- **You're doing <4 sessions/week for 2 weeks running.** The plan is wrong for your life; shrink the daily session to 20 min and put the accountability mechanism in place before pushing harder. Consistency at low intensity beats inconsistency at high intensity by a wide margin.
- **You hit 1500 before October.** Nice. Either push toward 1600, or hold and add chess variety — playing longer time controls, studying full games, working on a specific opening properly.

## Anti-patterns — the temptations specific to you

- **Watching streamers instead of playing.** It's fun and feels like studying. It isn't. Cap it at weekends, and only if you've done the day's session.
- **Playing blitz/bullet because rapid is slow.** Blitz at 800 will actively keep you at 800. You don't have the patterns fast enough for blitz to do anything but reinforce bad moves. Rapid only until 1400+.
- **Opening rabbit holes.** You will be tempted to spend hours on the Caro-Kann main lines. Don't. Through 1500, opening depth is almost zero ROI compared to tactics and endgames.
- **Skipping post-game analysis because the game was embarrassing.** The losses are where the signal is. Every loss you don't analyze is free rating points you left on the table.
- **Re-playing puzzles you've already solved because they feel good.** If it's fluent, it's not training. Keep the difficulty at the edge — where you get ~70% right, not 95%.
- **Declaring a phase done because you had one great day.** Rating is stochastic. A 1000-rated player is not one who peaked at 1000 once; it's one whose last 30 games averaged ~1000. Hold the phase bar until the rating is stable.

## On the feel of this plan

It will feel frustrating and slow in weeks 2–4. The blunder-check routine makes you play worse for 2 weeks before you play better — you're fighting an ingrained reflex. You will also feel, around phase 3, that you're "getting worse" as your opponents become the kind who don't blunder. That's actually you moving up into a new pool. Trust the process and keep the journal.

The 1500 target is realistic for 8–9 months at this cadence if you execute the daily session consistently. It becomes unrealistic the moment you skip a phase's foundation to chase the next one.
