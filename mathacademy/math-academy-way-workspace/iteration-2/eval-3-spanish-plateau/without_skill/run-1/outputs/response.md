Okay, take a breath. Nothing you've described sounds broken. It sounds like a very specific, very common, very fixable pattern. Let me unpack it, because I think you're actually diagnosing yourself a little wrong, and that's part of what's making it feel worse than it is.

## What's actually happening

A few things at once, and they're compounding:

**1. You've left the "beginner high" and hit the intermediate wall.** The first two months of a language feel magical because you go from 0 to being able to do *anything*. Every week you can say a new category of thing. That slope is not sustainable — not because you're failing, but because the amount of language required to climb the next rung gets larger at every rung. At month 3–4, the gap between "I learned a new tense today" and "I can feel that tense in my mouth during a live conversation" opens up. People almost universally interpret this as regression. It isn't. It's the first plateau, and everyone who gets good at a language walks through it.

**2. Your tutor is right: your grammar is improving. Your *retrieval under pressure* is not.** These are different skills and they plateau on different schedules. Anki builds recognition (passive). Lessons build production (active). Dreaming Spanish builds parsing (passive listening). None of these directly trains the specific skill of "pull a word you know out of your head in real time while a human is staring at you and your working memory is full." That skill is its own thing and it's probably undertrained in your current routine.

**3. The "I understand less than a month ago" thing is almost certainly not real.** This is one of the most well-documented illusions in language learning. What's happening: a month ago you were pattern-matching a handful of familiar words and filling in gaps with vibes, and you felt great. Now your ear is sharper, so you're *noticing* all the things you don't catch. Your comprehension went up; your awareness of the gap went up faster. It feels like regression. It isn't. (You can sanity-check this: re-watch a Dreaming Spanish video from 8 weeks ago that felt hard then. It will feel embarrassingly easy now.)

**4. The brain-freeze in lessons is partly cognitive and partly emotional.** When you're anxious about performing, working memory collapses, and the first thing to go is lexical retrieval in your weaker language. This is a known effect, it has nothing to do with whether you "know" the word, and the fact that it's been getting worse suggests the anxiety is now feeding itself — you freeze, you notice you froze, next lesson you're bracing for the freeze, etc.

**5. 200 Anki cards a day at month 4 is a lot.** That's probably ~45–75 minutes of reviews alone depending on card type and maturity, plus new cards. Combined with a weekly lesson and 30 min of input, you are doing real work every single day with no light days. Some of what you're calling a plateau may just be cognitive fatigue narrowing your bandwidth for the hard parts (speaking, listening).

So: you are not burnt out in the clinical sense, your approach is not wrong, and nothing is specifically broken. But your *mix* is out of balance for where you are now, and one specific skill (real-time production) is undertrained relative to the others.

## What to actually do

Here's a concrete prescription. Give it 3 weeks before you judge it.

**Rebalance Anki, don't abandon it.**
- Cap new cards at 10/day (or pause new cards entirely for 2 weeks — reviews only).
- Your daily review load should drop to 20–40 min within a week or two. That's the target.
- If most of your deck is single-word vocab cards, start converting (or adding) **sentence cards** — a full Spanish sentence on the front, audio if possible, English on the back. Sentence cards train the retrieval-in-context skill that your brain-freeze symptom is pointing at. Pure vocab cards build a dictionary you can't access under pressure.

**Add cheap output reps between tutor sessions.** This is the biggest lever. Once a week of speaking is not enough volume for production to keep up with your input and grammar. Options, pick one or two:
- **Self-talk / narration, 10 min a day.** Out loud, describe what you're doing, what you did today, what you'll do tomorrow. Sounds stupid, works absurdly well. You'll hit a wall on a word, look it up, and that word will stick because you needed it. This is the single highest-ROI thing you can add.
- **Journal in Spanish, 5–10 min a day.** Messy, no dictionary on the first pass. Then go back and look up the 3–5 things you couldn't say. Those become Anki cards (as sentences).
- **A second short italki session per week**, even 30 min, with a conversation tutor (not a teacher — cheaper, and you want reps, not grammar correction right now).

**Change how you use Dreaming Spanish for 2 weeks.** Stop chasing harder videos. Drop down one level from where you've been watching. Watch stuff that feels slightly too easy. This rebuilds the "I understand Spanish" feeling that you've lost, and it's also genuinely more useful — you want high comprehension with automaticity, not strained comprehension. Also: it is completely fine to rewatch videos. Rewatching is underrated.

**Fix the lesson freeze directly.** Tell your tutor explicitly, next lesson: "I'm freezing up and forgetting words I know. For the next few lessons I want to do lower-pressure speaking — can we do more describing-pictures, storytelling from prompts, or just chatting about my week, instead of targeted grammar practice?" A good tutor will happily pivot. Reducing the performance stakes for 3–4 sessions usually unsticks the freeze. Also: it's fine to pause, think, and say "¿cómo se dice…?" out loud in Spanish. That's not failing the lesson; that's the lesson.

**Take one full day off per week.** No Anki, no video, nothing. This is not indulgence, it's consolidation. People who study 7 days a week at month 4 often progress slower than people who study 6, because sleep and downtime are when the representations actually settle. Your "I feel demoralized" signal is partly telling you this.

## What to expect

If you do this, here's roughly what happens:
- Week 1: you'll feel slightly guilty doing less Anki. The freeze in lessons won't be fully gone but will be less bad because you've explicitly lowered the stakes.
- Week 2: self-talk or journaling starts paying off — you'll notice specific words coming out faster. Listening feels easier because you dropped the difficulty.
- Week 3: the "stuck" feeling is usually mostly gone. Not because you broke through some wall, but because you rebalanced and the production skill caught up a bit.

If after 3 weeks of this you still feel stuck in the same specific way, *then* it's worth looking harder at whether something structural is off. But I'd bet real money you won't.

## One last thing

Four months in, doing Anki daily, weekly lessons, near-daily input — you are doing more than 95% of people who say they're "learning Spanish." The demoralization is not evidence that it's not working. It's evidence that you've been pushing hard enough, long enough, to hit the first real plateau, which is a milestone, not a failure. The people who get fluent are just the ones who recognize this moment for what it is and adjust, instead of quitting or grinding harder on the thing that stopped scaling. You're at the adjust step. That's all this is.

Pick two things from the prescription above — I'd suggest (a) cap Anki + add sentence cards, and (b) 10 min of self-talk daily — and start tomorrow. Report back in 3 weeks.
