## Detecting the subject type

Most subjects fit one primary type but may have secondary roles (a politician who's also a lawyer, a scientist who's also a founder). Use the primary public role for the main checklist and pull from secondary types for relevant sub-sections.

When the type isn't obvious, choose based on what the person is most likely to be researched for — usually their most-publicly-known role. A retired athlete who entered politics is researched primarily as a politician now, with athlete background as biographical context.

## Politician

Holders or seekers of elected office, party officials, ministers, legislators, councillors.

**Tier-1 sources**:

- Election commission filings (ECI in India, FEC in US, Electoral Commission in UK, equivalents per country)
- Court orders and judgments (lower courts through supreme courts)
- Parliamentary / assembly records (Hansard, Lok Sabha / Rajya Sabha records, state assembly records)
- Official government notifications and gazettes
- Cabinet records where public
- Asset disclosure filings
- CAG / state audit reports referencing the person's portfolio

**Topic checklist**:

1. Personal background — birth, family, regional roots
2. Education
3. Pre-political career
4. Entry into politics — first party, mentor, first contest
5. Constituencies represented (every term)
6. Ministerial / leadership roles held
7. Major legislative positions taken (votes, bills sponsored)
8. Defections / party switches with dates and stated reasons
9. Electoral history — every contest with constituency, party, vote share, margin, opponent
10. Controversies and legal cases — allegations / charges / convictions
11. Stated ideology and notable policy positions
12. Family members in politics (dynasty considerations)
13. Recent activity (last 24 months) — campaigns, key speeches, public stances
14. Public perception — academic / journalistic analysis

**Type-specific gotchas**:

- Allegation / charge / conviction discipline matters most here. Politicians attract accusations; a research file that doesn't distinguish them is unfair both to the subject and to the reader.
- ECI affidavits are sworn declarations and treated as Tier 1, but cross-reference with reporting since omissions occur.
- Party-aligned outlets vary between Tier 2 and Tier 3 depending on track record on factual reporting versus opinion.

## Business executive

CEOs, CFOs, founders, board members, senior corporate leaders.

**Tier-1 sources**:

- SEC filings (US), MCA filings (India), Companies House (UK), regional equivalents
- Annual reports and proxy statements
- Earnings call transcripts
- Court filings in commercial litigation
- Regulatory enforcement records (SEC, FTC, SEBI, CCI, FCA, etc.)
- Bankruptcy filings where applicable
- Patent filings naming the person

**Topic checklist**:

1. Personal background and education
2. Career trajectory — every senior role with dates
3. Companies founded or led
4. Board positions held (current and historical)
5. Compensation and equity holdings (where disclosed)
6. Notable strategic decisions and outcomes
7. Regulatory or legal entanglements
8. Industry impact — how peers and analysts assess
9. Philanthropy and political donations
10. Recent activity (last 24 months)

**Type-specific gotchas**:

- Self-published profiles (LinkedIn, personal sites) are Tier 4 — verify against filings.
- Crunchbase / Pitchbook are useful Tier 3 sources for deal data.
- Distinguish operating-company decisions from personal positions of the executive.

## Academic / scientist

Professors, researchers, scientists in universities or research institutions.

**Tier-1 sources**:

- Peer-reviewed publications (Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, ORCID profile)
- Citation indices (h-index, total citations) — note which database
- Grant databases (NIH RePORTER, NSF awards, ERC, equivalents)
- Institutional CV and faculty pages
- Patent records
- Conference proceedings
- Editorial / review board memberships
- Retraction records (Retraction Watch)

**Topic checklist**:

1. Personal background and education (degrees, institutions, advisors)
2. Career — every academic appointment with dates
3. Field of research and major contributions
4. Major publications (top 10–20 by citation)
5. Funding history
6. Students supervised (PhD students often listed in CVs / Mathematics Genealogy Project)
7. Awards and honours
8. Editorial / society leadership roles
9. Public engagement (op-eds, popular books, media appearances)
10. Any retractions, misconduct findings, or disputes

**Type-specific gotchas**:

- h-index and citation counts vary across databases — cite the database used.
- Distinguish first-author / corresponding-author / co-author contributions.
- Field norms for authorship vary (humanities vs. life sciences vs. CS) — note the convention.

## Athlete

Active or retired professional athletes.

**Tier-1 sources**:

- Governing-body official records (FIFA, IOC, ICC, FIDE, ATP/WTA, NBA, equivalents)
- League / competition official sites for stats and records
- Anti-doping registries (WADA, USADA, NADA)
- Disciplinary records from governing bodies
- Court records for any legal matters

**Topic checklist**:

1. Personal background and early career
2. Career trajectory — clubs / teams / events with dates
3. Major titles and records
4. Statistical record (sport-specific key metrics)
5. National team / international representation
6. Coaching / training history
7. Injury history (where reported)
8. Anti-doping / disciplinary history
9. Endorsements and business interests
10. Post-career activity (if retired)

**Type-specific gotchas**:

- Stat sites (ESPN Cricinfo, Transfermarkt, Baseball-Reference) sit between Tier 1 and Tier 2 — generally Tier 1 for stats, Tier 2 for analysis.
- Doping suspensions vary in disclosure depending on jurisdiction and substance.

## Artist / entertainer

Actors, musicians, directors, fiction writers, visual artists, performers.

**Tier-1 sources**:

- IMDb (filmography), Discogs (discography), official label / studio sites
- Awards body records (Academy / Grammys / BAFTA / Filmfare / Booker, etc.)
- Copyright and royalty databases (ASCAP, BMI, equivalents)
- Court records for IP / contract / personal litigation
- Auction house records for visual artists

**Topic checklist**:

1. Personal background and early life
2. Training and influences
3. Career chronology — every major work / role with dates
4. Awards and nominations
5. Critical reception (notable reviews, retrospectives)
6. Commercial performance where relevant (box office, sales, gallery prices)
7. Collaborations and creative circle
8. Business interests, ownership, production companies
9. Activism / public political stances
10. Personal life events that became public

**Type-specific gotchas**:

- IMDb is user-edited — Tier 2, not Tier 1, for biographical claims (Tier 1 only for filmography).
- Distinguish performance / writing / directing / producing credits.
- Tabloid coverage is Tier 4 unless replicated by Tier-2 outlets.

## Religious figure

Heads of orders, theologians, prominent monks / clergy / imams / rabbis, sect founders.

**Tier-1 sources**:

- Institutional records of the order / denomination / sect
- Published theological / philosophical writings
- Encyclical / papal documents (for Catholic figures)
- Court filings where applicable (institutional litigation, abuse cases, financial)
- Government registries for religious trusts where public

**Topic checklist**:

1. Personal background and early religious formation
2. Lineage / parampara / ordination
3. Institutional roles held
4. Theological / philosophical contributions
5. Major writings, sermons, public addresses
6. Followers, students, designated successors
7. Sectarian disputes or schisms
8. Institutional governance and finance
9. Allegations and any legal matters
10. Inter-faith engagement and political alignment

**Type-specific gotchas**:

- Hagiographic sources are common — treat institutional biographies as Tier 3 unless cross-verified.
- Dates and lineage in traditional records may use traditional calendars — note this.
- Allegations against religious figures often surface in Tier 2 long after the fact — search recent reporting.

## Activist / movement leader

Civil rights leaders, environmental activists, labour organisers, social movement figures.

**Tier-1 sources**:

- Movement archives (institutional or academic-curated)
- Court filings — both as plaintiff (rights cases) and as defendant (criminal)
- Government records of arrests / detention / surveillance (where declassified)
- Speeches, manifestos, foundational writings
- Truth commission reports where applicable

**Topic checklist**:

1. Personal background
2. Path into activism — formative events, early organising
3. Movement(s) led or affiliated with
4. Major campaigns and outcomes
5. Tactics and ideology
6. Imprisonment / detention / persecution history
7. Allies, collaborators, rival movements
8. Writings and speeches
9. Recognition (awards, honours, official rehabilitation if posthumous)
10. Legacy and historical assessment

**Type-specific gotchas**:

- State sources may be hostile to the subject — read against the grain.
- Hagiographic movement sources may overstate centrality — cross-check with independent academic accounts.

## Historical figure (deceased >50 years)

Subjects whose lives are largely closed and where research relies on archives and scholarship rather than current reporting.

**Tier-1 sources**:

- Primary archival documents (letters, diaries, official records of the period)
- Government archives (national archives, presidential libraries, parliamentary records)
- Scholarly biographies (peer-reviewed academic works)
- Period newspapers (digitised archives)
- Estate / family papers in academic collections

**Topic checklist**:

1. Birth, family, social context of the era
2. Education and formative influences
3. Career and major events
4. Relationships and correspondence
5. Writings and public statements
6. Historical impact and legacy
7. Posthumous reputation evolution
8. Major biographers and their interpretive lenses
9. Historiographical debates
10. Modern memorialisation or controversy

**Type-specific gotchas**:

- Web search alone is insufficient — academic databases (JSTOR, Project MUSE, archive.org's scholarly collection) are essential. Note when these aren't accessible.
- Distinguish between primary sources, contemporary secondary sources (early biographies), and modern scholarly work.
- Note when claims rest on a single biographer vs. consensus.

## Journalist / writer

Reporters, columnists, editors, non-fiction authors, public intellectuals.

**Tier-1 sources**:

- Bylines aggregated across employers
- Books published with publisher / ISBN / dates
- Journalism awards (Pulitzer, Magsaysay, RTDNA, equivalents)
- Press freedom / safety records (CPJ, RSF) for journalists at risk
- Court filings — defamation cases, source-protection cases

**Topic checklist**:

1. Personal background and education
2. Journalism career — outlets and beats
3. Major reporting / investigations / books
4. Awards and recognition
5. Editorial roles held
6. Notable sources / scoops
7. Litigation, retractions, corrections
8. Press freedom incidents (detentions, threats)
9. Public commentary and political alignment
10. Influence on policy / discourse

**Type-specific gotchas**:

- Self-byline pages may omit retracted or contested pieces — search corrections logs.
- Distinguish reporting from opinion writing in the body of work.

## Tech founder

Startup founders, especially in software, hardware, biotech, fintech.

**Tier-1 sources**:

- Incorporation records (Companies House, MCA, state registries)
- Patent filings
- Funding databases (Crunchbase, Pitchbook — Tier 1 for deal data, Tier 3 for narrative)
- Court records in IP / contract litigation
- SEC filings post-IPO

**Topic checklist**:

1. Personal background and education
2. Pre-founding career
3. Companies founded — every venture with dates, role, outcome
4. Funding history (rounds, valuations, investors)
5. Products / technologies
6. Acquisitions, IPOs, exits
7. Board roles and advisory positions
8. Investments / angel activity
9. Public commentary on tech / policy
10. Litigation and controversies

**Type-specific gotchas**:

- Company narratives change over time — pin claims to dates rather than letting current framing colour past events.
- "Founder" titles are sometimes retrofitted — verify against earliest funding documents.
- Founder / founding-team disputes are common — present multiple accounts where they exist.

---

## Other / mixed

For subjects who don't cleanly fit a type, build the topic checklist by combining the most relevant types. Common combinations:

- Politician + lawyer: politician checklist + bar council records, major cases argued.
- Scientist + entrepreneur: academic checklist + tech-founder sources.
- Athlete + politician (post-career entry to politics): athlete + politician.
- Artist + activist: artist + activist.
- Religious figure + politician (clergy in elected office): religious figure + politician.

When in doubt about Tier 1 sources, the test is: is this an authoritative primary record (governmental, institutional, peer-reviewed) or is it secondary commentary? Primary records are Tier 1.
