# You're not broken. Your plan is.

Short version first, then I'll show my work:

You're not burnt out, and this isn't just "what plateaus feel like." You're experiencing two separate, diagnosable things at once, and they're both fixable:

1. **Your speaking freezes are a retrieval-practice problem, not a knowledge problem.** You've been putting Spanish *in* (Anki, Dreaming Spanish, tutor explanations) far more than you've been pulling it *out* under pressure. Your tutor is right that your grammar has improved — on paper. But grammar you recognize is not grammar you can produce in real time, and you haven't been training the produce-in-real-time muscle.
2. **Your listening feeling worse is almost certainly a noticing effect, not regression.** You now hear the gaps you used to be deaf to. This is a very common month-3-to-4 experience and is actually a progress signal, not a regression signal. I'll explain how to verify it below.

Neither of these means your 4 months were wasted. They mean you hit the ceiling of a specific input-heavy approach and need to rebalance.

---

## What's actually happening (diagnosis)

Let me break down your three activities and what each one is and isn't training.

**Anki (200 cards/day).** This is building *recognition* — can you, when shown a Spanish word, produce its meaning? It builds a passive vocabulary. It does not, by itself, build the ability to retrieve a Spanish word when you need it in a sentence. Those are genuinely different skills using different neural pathways. Worse: 200 cards/day is a *lot*, and if your retention on them is anywhere below ~85%, you're spending a large chunk of your daily Spanish time on cards that aren't actually sticking, which eats into the time you could spend producing.

**Dreaming Spanish (30 min/day).** Comprehensible input. Excellent for building your ear, your intuitive sense of grammar, and your passive vocabulary. But input alone, no matter how many hours, is *notorious* for producing learners who understand a lot and can't speak. This is a documented pattern — there are long-time DS watchers with 1000+ hours of input who still freeze up in conversation. Input is necessary; it is not sufficient.

**Weekly 1-hour iTalki lesson.** This is your only production practice, and it's 60 minutes out of roughly 750 minutes of Spanish per week — about 8%. That is way too low a production ratio for the level of output you're expecting from yourself. The cognitive-science rule of thumb is that active production should be ~3x consumption for intermediate learners, and closer to 5-6x for people who feel they're "underperforming relative to input." You're running it at roughly 1:10 in the wrong direction.

So here's the mechanical reason your brain freezes in lessons:

- You have a big passive vocabulary (Anki + DS have built this).
- You have decent grammatical intuitions (tutor confirms this).
- You have essentially zero automaticity at *pulling words off the shelf under time pressure*, because the only time you ever practice that is during the one hour a week when the stakes are highest.

Freezing in your tutor session isn't a sign that you don't know the words. It's a sign that "know the word" (recognition) and "produce the word in under 2 seconds while someone looks at you" (retrieval + automaticity) are two skills, and you've only been training one of them.

**About the listening thing specifically.** Feeling like you understand *less* than a month ago is almost always one of two things:

1. **Noticing effect (most likely).** Around month 3-4, learners start hearing individual words and grammatical structures that they were previously hearing as a blur. Paradoxically this *feels like* understanding less, because you're now aware of all the specific pieces you're missing, rather than getting a fuzzy gist and moving on. This is a sign of progress being integrated, not lost.
2. **Genuine review-starvation.** If the DS videos have shifted up in level, or you're listening more passively (phone in pocket while doing dishes), you may simply be consuming content that's now above your comfortable comprehension level without the review mechanism catching up.

Quick test: go back and rewatch a DS video you watched 6 weeks ago. If you understand it substantially more easily than you did the first time, you haven't regressed — you're in the noticing phase. If it feels the same or harder, something else is going on.

**It's probably not burnout.** Burnout looks like: dreading the sessions, skipping them, performance slipping on *easy* material you used to ace, and disturbed sleep/mood. What you're describing is specifically a performance feeling, not an energy-and-dread feeling. You're still showing up, you're still doing the cards, you still want this. That's not burnout. That's a well-motivated learner running a plan with a structural flaw.

The demoralization is real and deserved given what it feels like from the inside. But the thing that's broken is specifiable and fixable, and you have not wasted 4 months — you've built the raw material (vocabulary, grammatical intuition, an ear for the sound shape of Spanish) that the next phase needs.

## What to change

The core move: **shift your ratio from ~90% input / 10% output to more like 50% input / 50% output, and add retrieval practice under time pressure every single day.**

Here's a revised daily and weekly structure. Your total time doesn't need to increase — the composition does.

### Revised daily session (~60-75 min on weekdays, adjust to your real budget)

**1. Anki, but cut in half (~15 min).**
Drop to 100 cards/day. I know this sounds like giving up — it's not. 200/day is almost certainly giving you a retention rate below 80%, and with that many cards, you're burning mental budget on flashcards instead of on output. Cap new cards at 10/day. Let the backlog drain for a week.

**More importantly: flip the direction of at least half your cards to English → Spanish (production direction).** Recognition cards (Spanish → English) are easier and feel more productive, which is why people default to them. Production cards (English → Spanish, where you have to say/type the Spanish from memory, with gender and conjugation correct) are much harder and train the exact skill you're missing. If your deck is all Spanish → English right now, that alone explains a lot of the speaking freeze.

**2. Speaking-by-yourself practice, daily (~15-20 min). THIS IS THE ONE YOU'RE MISSING.**
Pick one of these each day and rotate:

- **Monologue recording.** Pick a prompt ("describe what I did yesterday," "explain my job to a kid," "what I think about X"). Speak for 2-3 minutes into your phone's voice memo. No notes. No pauses to look stuff up — just power through and make mistakes out loud. Then listen back once. Write down the 3 specific things you couldn't say or got wrong. Those go straight into Anki as new production cards.
- **Shadowing.** Play a DS segment, pause every sentence, and repeat it out loud trying to match the prosody and speed. This is how you build the articulatory fluency that makes your mouth not feel like it's wading through glue.
- **Written journaling under time pressure.** Set a 10-minute timer. Write about your day in Spanish, no dictionary, no pauses. When the timer hits, stop. Then — and only then — look up the words you didn't know and make Anki cards from them.

The point of all three: you have to *produce, from memory, under time pressure, without a safety net*. This feels awful for the first 2 weeks. That's the training signal — it means you're finally exercising the retrieval pathway you've been avoiding.

**3. Dreaming Spanish, but with a production tail (~20-25 min).**
Keep watching — it's a good resource — but stop watching passively. Every 2-3 minutes, pause the video and summarize out loud in Spanish what was just said. It halves the amount of input you get through and roughly quadruples what you learn from each minute, because you're converting consumption into retrieval practice. If you're listening while cooking/walking, that's fine — but count that time as entertainment, not study, and don't let it replace active input sessions.

**4. Tutor session preparation, weekly (~15 min, day before the lesson).**
Write down in English 5-10 things you want to say this week in the lesson. Actually try to say them out loud in Spanish before the lesson. This turns your weekly lesson from "improvise and freeze" into "attempt specific things and get corrected on specific things."

### Revised weekly structure

- **Monday-Saturday:** the daily session above.
- **Sunday (or whenever):** a light day. Anki + a DS video, that's it. You need a recovery day; not taking one is a slow-burn contributor to the "feeling stuck" sensation.
- **iTalki lesson:** keep it, but restructure. Ask your tutor to do **at least 30 minutes of the hour as free conversation with you initiating topics**, not tutor-led explanation. Tutor explanations of grammar are the least valuable thing a tutor does for you right now — you can get grammar explanations from a book. What you can only get from a tutor is forcing-function conversation practice with immediate correction. Tell the tutor explicitly: "I want to talk more and be corrected, and I want less grammar explanation." Good tutors will adapt.
- **Once every 2-3 weeks:** add a second conversation session. Could be a cheap iTalki "community tutor" (conversation practice, not certified teachers, usually half the price), or a language exchange on Tandem/HelloTalk. More spoken reps in more varied registers is what you need.

### Give it 3 weeks before judging

This will feel **worse** before it feels better. Specifically:

- Week 1-2: your monologue recordings will be painful to listen to. You'll notice how bad you sound. This is not new information — it's new *awareness* of information that was always true.
- Around week 3: something will unlock in a tutor session. A sentence will come out fluently that wouldn't have before. That's the signal the retrieval pathway is starting to form.
- By week 6-8: your tutor will tell you, unprompted, that you're speaking more fluidly.

If by **week 4** you genuinely see no improvement in the freezing, then we revisit — probably by cutting Anki further, adding more speaking, or checking whether there's a specific grammar gap (e.g., you may be freezing on verb conjugations specifically, in which case a focused 2-week conjugation sprint would help).

## One hard thing I want to name

You have a weekly tutor. That's already your best possible accountability structure, and you're underusing it. The single highest-leverage change in this whole response is probably:

**Tell your tutor this week, explicitly: "I want to speak more and be corrected more, and I want less explanation. I don't care if it's uncomfortable. Push me."**

Tutors default to explanation mode because most students are conflict-avoidant about being corrected, and because explaining feels productive to both parties. You need the other mode. Ask for it. And — this is the key — bring a list of things you noticed yourself struggling with this week (from your daily recordings) and ask the tutor to drill you on those specifically. That turns the tutor session from "input" into "targeted remediation of your actual current weak spots," which is where almost all of the value of a tutor lives.
